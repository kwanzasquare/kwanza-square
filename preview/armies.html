The two armies

Built from Martin's photograph rather than from a description of it: a standing figure with a round featureless head, a long neck, two arms, two legs and feet on the floor. No face, because the reference has none and because at playing size two dots read as a speck of dirt.

The two sides differ in shape as well as colour — colour alone fails anyone colour-blind, anyone on a dim phone, and anyone across a room from a television. One stands with both arms down; the other rests a hand on its hip, and that triangle of daylight under the elbow is still recognisable long after the colour has stopped being.

First army — both arms down, spear at the right side, plain headring.

Second army — left hand on the hip, knobkerrie, single feather.
